# Case: Tesla Model X with Autopilot (Mountain View, California, 2018)

Source: NTSB Highway Accident Report HAR-20/01. This is a description of the system and its use before the crash. It does not include the investigation findings.

## System
A 2017 Tesla Model X has Autopilot, a SAE Level 2 partial driving automation feature. It combines Traffic-Aware Cruise Control (TACC) and Autosteer (lane keeping). The driver stays responsible for driving and monitoring the road.

## Main parts
- Sensors: a forward-facing camera, a forward-facing radar and ultrasonic sensors.
- Vision processing: a camera-based system detects lane markings, vehicles and some objects ahead.
- TACC: holds a speed set by the driver, follows a vehicle ahead at a chosen gap, and returns to the set speed when no vehicle is ahead.
- Autosteer: keeps the vehicle in its lane using lane markings and the path of the vehicle ahead.
- Forward collision warning and automatic emergency braking are available as separate features.
- Driver monitoring: steering torque is used to check that the driver's hands are on the wheel, with visual and audible alerts if no hands are detected for a period of time.

## People
- The driver sets the cruising speed, turns Autopilot on, and is told to keep hands on the wheel and watch the road.
- Other road users and road workers share the highway. Road agencies maintain road markings and safety hardware such as barriers and crash attenuators.

## Operating context
Busy multi-lane freeway with high-occupancy-vehicle (HOV) lanes, exits and interchanges, lane markings of different quality, and traffic that changes in speed.
